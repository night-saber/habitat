# Habitat — Trade Research & Terminology Guide

Comprehensive research on 6 trades: Plumbing, Electrical, HVAC, Roofing, Landscaping, General Contracting.

## 1. PLUMBING

### Common Homeowner Complaints → Trade Translation
| Homeowner Says | Trade Term / What It Means |
|---|---|
| "Toilet keeps running" | Worn flapper valve or faulty fill valve — replace flapper, check fill valve height |
| "Water won't stop running" | Valve stem/cartridge failure — replace cartridge or valve stem |
| "No hot water" | Heating element failure (electric) or thermocouple/gas valve (gas) — check element, thermostat, anode rod |
| "Sink is clogged" | Drain blockage in P-trap or downstream — clear with snake or plunger, inspect P-trap |
| "Low water pressure" | PRV (pressure reducing valve) failure, aerator clog, or pipe corrosion — check PRV (should be 40-60 PSI), clean aerator |
| "Leaking pipe" | Pipe joint failure, corrosion, or freeze damage — locate source, repair/replace section |
| "Leak under sink" | Supply line, drain connection, or P-trap failure — inspect supply lines, drain connections, P-trap |
| "Water heater making noise" | Sediment buildup in tank — flush tank, check anode rod |
| "Sewer smell" | Dry P-trap, vent stack blockage, or sewer line issue — check trap water seal, inspect vent stack |
| "Water is discolored" | Corroded pipes, water heater sediment, or municipal supply issue — flush system, check anode rod |
| "Toilet won't flush" | Flush valve failure, wax ring leak, or low water level — check flush valve, wax ring, fill valve |
| "Shower pressure is bad" | Shower valve cartridge failure, clogged showerhead, or PRV issue — replace cartridge, clean/replace head |
| "Pipe is frozen" | Frozen pipe section — thaw with hair dryer/heating pad, NEVER open flame |
| "Sewage backup" | Main sewer line blockage or damage — call professional immediately, do not use fixtures |

### Key Terminology
- **P-trap**: U-shaped pipe under sink that holds water to block sewer gases
- **Flapper**: Rubber valve in toilet tank that releases water for flushing
- **Fill valve**: Toilet component that refills tank after flush
- **Cartridge**: Replaceable valve stem assembly in faucets
- **Anode rod**: Sacrificial metal rod in water heater that prevents tank corrosion
- **T&P valve**: Temperature & Pressure relief valve on water heater (safety device)
- **PRV**: Pressure Reducing Valve — maintains 40-60 PSI household pressure
- **Backflow preventer**: Device preventing contaminated water from entering clean supply
- **Vent stack**: Vertical pipe that balances air pressure and vents sewer gases
- **Supply line**: Pipe bringing water to fixture
- **Water closet**: Toilet (technical term)
- **Lavatory**: Bathroom sink (technical term)
- **Hose bib**: Outdoor faucet/spigot

### Tools
Pipe wrench, basin wrench, adjustable wrench, channel-lock pliers, plunger, drain snake (hand-crank & powered), pipe cutter, propane torch, Teflon tape (PTFE), plumber's putty, pipe dope, silicone caulk, pressure gauge, flashlight, bucket

### Safety
- Always shut off water supply before repairs
- Never use open flame to thaw frozen pipes
- Electrical near water = shock hazard — use GFCI protection
- Gas line work requires licensed professional
- Scalding risk from hot water systems
- Chemical drain cleaners can damage pipes and cause burns
- Wear safety goggles and gloves

### Permits
- Required for: new water/drain lines, water heater install, sewer work, bathroom remodels
- Not required for: fixture replacement, minor repairs, unclogging
- Cost: $50-$500+ depending on jurisdiction and scope
- Per-fixture fees common ($10-$25 per fixture)

### Pricing
- Service call: $75-$150
- Hourly: $45-$200/hr
- Per fixture: $100-$500
- Water heater replacement: $800-$2,500
- Sewer line repair: $1,000-$5,000+

### Seasonal
- **Winter**: Frozen/burst pipes — insulate exposed pipes, disconnect hoses, keep heat above 55°F, let faucets drip
- **Summer**: Heavy water use strains system — check washing machine hoses, inspect sprinklers, monitor water heater
- **Spring**: Inspect outdoor faucets, check sprinkler systems, flush water heater
- **Fall**: Winterize outdoor plumbing, drain sprinkler systems, insulate pipes

## 2. ELECTRICAL

### Common Homeowner Complaints → Trade Translation
| Homeowner Says | Trade Term / What It Means |
|---|---|
| "Outlet not working" | Dead receptacle — test GFCI, check wiring, verify breaker |
| "No power" | Tripped breaker, GFCI trip, or service issue — check panel, test GFCI |
| "Light flickering" | Loose connection, failing ballast, or dimmer incompatibility — check wiring, ballast, dimmer |
| "Breaker keeps tripping" | Circuit overload, short circuit, or ground fault — inspect load, check for shorts |
| "Sparking" | Loose connection or failing device — de-energize immediately, inspect connections |
| "Dead outlet" | GFCI trip, wiring failure, or backstab failure — test GFCI, check wiring |
| "Ceiling fan noisy" | Loose blades, failing motor, or bad capacitor — tighten blades, check motor/capacitor |
| "Light won't turn on" | Bad switch, fixture, or wiring — test switch, fixture, wiring |
| "Outlet is warm/hot" | Overloaded circuit or loose connection — reduce load, inspect connections immediately |
| "Buzzing sound" | Loose connection, failing device, or arcing — de-energize, inspect immediately |
| "Burning smell" | Overheating wire or device — EMERGENCY: shut off power, call electrician |
| "Lights dim when appliance starts" | Voltage drop from high-draw appliance — check circuit capacity, possible dedicated circuit needed |

### Key Terminology
- **GFCI**: Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter — protects against shock in wet areas
- **AFCI**: Arc Fault Circuit Interrupter — protects against arc faults/fire
- **Circuit breaker**: Automatic switch that trips on overload/short
- **Receptacle**: Outlet (technical term)
- **Switch leg**: Wire from switch to fixture
- **Pigtail**: Short wire connecting multiple wires to a device
- **Wire gauge (AWG)**: Wire thickness — lower number = thicker wire
- **Conduit**: Protective tubing for wires
- **Junction box**: Enclosure for wire connections
- **Panel/Breaker panel**: Distribution center for electrical circuits
- **Service entrance**: Where power enters the home
- **Load calculation**: Determining electrical demand for sizing
- **Backstab**: Push-in wire connection (less reliable than screw terminals)
- **Tamper-resistant receptacle**: Outlet with built-in shutter safety mechanism

### Tools
Multimeter, non-contact voltage tester, wire strippers, fish tape, circuit tester, GFCI tester, insulated screwdrivers, needle-nose pliers, lineman's pliers, cable ripper, drywall saw

### Safety
- ALWAYS turn off power at breaker before working
- Test before touching — use non-contact voltage tester
- Lockout/tagout for panel work
- Never work in wet conditions or with wet hands
- Burning smell, sparking, or smoke = EMERGENCY — shut off power, call professional
- Panel work should be left to licensed electricians
- Never overload circuits or use extension cords as permanent wiring
- Use insulated tools and wear insulated gloves
- Water + electricity = lethal combination

### Permits
- Required for: new circuits, panel upgrades, new construction, major renovations
- Not required for: fixture replacement, switch/outlet replacement (like-for-like)
- Cost: $50-$500+ depending on jurisdiction
- Flat fee common for residential ($50-$75 for single-family)

### Pricing
- Service call: $75-$150
- Hourly: $50-$150/hr
- Per outlet/switch: $50-$150
- Panel upgrade: $1,000-$3,000
- New circuit: $150-$500

### Seasonal
- **Summer**: AC load peak — check cooling systems, avoid overloading circuits
- **Winter**: heating load, holiday lighting — inspect space heaters, check GFCI
- **Spring**: irrigation systems, outdoor lighting — service outdoor circuits
- **Fall**: prepare for heating season, inspect electrical heat systems

## 3. HVAC

### Common Homeowner Complaints → Trade Translation
| Homeowner Says | Trade Term / What It Means |
|---|---|
| "No AC" | Compressor failure, refrigerant leak, capacitor failure, or thermostat issue — check compressor, refrigerant, capacitor, thermostat |
| "AC not cold" | Low refrigerant, dirty condenser coil, or failing compressor — check refrigerant, clean condenser, inspect compressor |
| "AC won't turn on" | Thermostat failure, capacitor failure, or wiring issue — test thermostat, capacitor, wiring |
| "No heat" | Ignitor failure, gas valve issue, or thermostat problem — check ignitor, gas valve, thermostat |
| "System is loud" | Loose components, failing blower motor, or debris in unit — inspect blower, check for loose parts |
| "High energy bills" | Dirty filter, low refrigerant, duct leaks, or aging system — replace filter, check refrigerant, seal ducts |
| "System short cycling" | Oversized unit, dirty filter, or refrigerant issue — check filter, verify sizing, check refrigerant |
| "Water leaking" | Clogged condensate drain or cracked drain pan — clear condensate line, inspect drain pan |
| "Strange smell" | Mold in ducts, gas leak, or dirty filter — inspect ducts, check gas connections, replace filter |
| "Vents blowing weak air" | Dirty filter, blocked ducts, or failing blower — replace filter, inspect ducts, check blower |
| "Ice on refrigerant lines" | Low refrigerant or airflow restriction — shut off system, check filter, call professional |
| "Thermostat not accurate" | Calibration issue, location problem, or dead batteries — recalibrate, relocate, replace batteries |

### Key Terminology
- **Condenser**: Outdoor unit that releases heat
- **Evaporator coil**: Indoor coil that absorbs heat
- **Compressor**: Heart of cooling cycle — compresses refrigerant
- **Blower motor**: Fan that circulates air through ducts
- **Capacitor**: Electrical component that helps start motors
- **Contactor**: Electrical switch that controls power to unit
- **Heat exchanger**: Metal chamber where combustion heat transfers to air
- **Refrigerant**: Chemical compound that absorbs and releases heat (R-410A, R-22)
- **SEER**: Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio — efficiency rating
- **BTU**: British Thermal Unit — heating/cooling capacity
- **Tonnage**: Cooling capacity (1 ton = 12,000 BTU/hr)
- **Condensate drain**: Pipe that removes moisture from AC
- **Air handler**: Indoor unit that circulates air
- **Manual J**: Load calculation for sizing HVAC equipment
- **Manual S**: Equipment selection based on Manual J

### Tools
Manifold gauge set, vacuum pump, multimeter, refrigerant leak detector, fin comb, coil cleaner, wet/dry vacuum, thermostat, insulated gloves, safety goggles

### Safety
- EPA Section 608 certification REQUIRED for refrigerant handling
- Electrical disconnect before service — 240V power is lethal
- Capacitors hold lethal charge even when power is off
- Lockout/tagout required
- Carbon monoxide risk from cracked heat exchanger
- Never vent refrigerant — illegal and hazardous
- Insulated gloves and eye protection required
- Hearing protection for brazing/compressor work

### Permits
- Required for: system replacement, new install, ductwork modification, gas line work
- Not required for: filter changes, capacitor replacement, contactor replacement, maintenance
- Cost: $75-$500+ (mechanical permit + electrical/gas sub-permits)
- ACCA Manual J load calculation required in most jurisdictions

### Pricing
- Service call: $75-$150
- Hourly: $85-$175/hr
- AC replacement: $3,000-$7,000
- Furnace replacement: $2,500-$6,000
- Full system: $5,000-$12,000+
- Ductwork: $500-$2,000

### Seasonal
- **Spring**: AC tune-up — clean condenser, check refrigerant, replace filter, test capacitor
- **Summer**: Peak demand — avoid scheduling, highest prices, emergency repairs
- **Fall**: Furnace tune-up — inspect heat exchanger, check ignitor, test CO detector, replace filter
- **Winter**: Heating emergencies — highest demand for furnace repairs

## 4. ROOFING

### Common Homeowner Complaints → Trade Translation
| Homeowner Says | Trade Term / What It Means |
|---|---|
| "Roof is leaking" | Failed flashing, damaged shingles, or compromised underlayment — locate source, repair flashing/shingles |
| "Leak in attic" | Roof penetration failure, flashing failure, or ice dam — inspect penetrations, flashing, check for ice dams |
| "Shingle is missing" | Wind damage or failed adhesive — replace shingle, check surrounding shingles |
| "Shingle is curled/cupped" | Age-related deterioration or poor ventilation — assess roof age, check attic ventilation |
| "Gutter is clogged" | Leaf/debris buildup — clear gutter, check downspout |
| "Gutter is falling" | Failed hangers or fascia damage — re-secure hangers, inspect fascia |
| "Roof is old" | Approaching end of service life — inspect deck, shingle condition, underlayment |
| "Ice dam on roof" | Heat loss through attic causing snow melt/refreeze — improve insulation and ventilation |
| "Moss/algae on roof" | Moisture retention — clean roof, improve ventilation, consider zinc/copper strips |
| "Sagging roof" | Structural issue with decking or rafters — professional structural assessment needed |
| "Granules in gutter" | Shingle deterioration — assess shingle age and condition |
| "Skylight leaking" | Failed skylight seal or flashing — inspect seal, repair flashing |
| "Chimney leak" | Failed chimney flashing or cap — inspect flashing, cap, crown |

### Key Terminology
- **Ridge**: Horizontal peak where two roof planes meet
- **Eave**: Lower roof edge, typically above gutter
- **Valley**: Inside angle where two roof planes meet (water channel)
- **Hip**: External angle where two roof planes meet
- **Rake**: Sloped edge of gable roof
- **Flashing**: Metal pieces that seal roof penetrations and transitions
- **Underlayment**: Waterproof barrier under shingles
- **Drip edge**: Metal edge that directs water into gutter
- **Starter shingles**: First row of shingles at eave
- **Ridge cap**: Shingles covering the ridge
- **Roof plane**: One flat surface/section of roof
- **Pitch/Slope**: Steepness of roof (e.g., 6/12 = 6" rise per 12" run)
- **Decking**: Sheathing (plywood/OSB) that shingles attach to
- **Pipe boot/pipe jack**: Flashing around vent pipes
- **Counterflashing**: Flashing that overlaps step flashing
- **Ice and water shield**: Self-adhering underlayment for valleys and eaves
- **Tear-off**: Removing old roofing material

### Tools
Roofing nailer, roofing shovel/tear-off tool, utility knife, chalk line, tin snips, caulk gun, pry bar, hammer, measuring tape, harness, ladder, safety rope

### Safety
- Fall protection harness required on slopes >6/12
- Ladder 4:1 angle rule (1 ft out for every 4 ft up)
- Power line awareness — maintain 10ft clearance
- Heat illness prevention in summer — hydrate, take breaks
- Never work in high winds, rain, or icy conditions
- PPE: gloves, eye protection, non-slip footwear, hard hat
- Roof jacks and planks for steep slopes
- Never walk on wet or mossy roof

### Permits
- Required for: full roof replacement, re-roofing, structural changes, material changes, skylights
- Not required for: minor repairs (<25% of roof), replacing few shingles, re-caulking flashing
- Cost: $150-$500+ (flat fee or percentage of project value)
- Some jurisdictions require structural engineering for material changes

### Pricing
- Per square (100 sq ft): $100-$400
- Full replacement: $5,000-$15,000+
- Repair: $150-$500
- Inspection: $100-$300
- Emergency tarping: $200-$500

### Seasonal
- **Spring**: Optimal repair season — mild temperatures, good material adhesion
- **Summer**: Peak demand — highest prices, longest wait times, heat safety concerns
- **Fall**: Second best season — good weather, pre-winter urgency, competitive pricing
- **Winter**: Emergency repairs only — material brittleness, safety risks, weather delays
- Material temperature requirements: asphalt shingles need 40°F+ for proper sealing

## 5. LANDSCAPING

### Common Homeowner Complaints → Trade Translation
| Homeowner Says | Trade Term / What It Means |
|---|---|
| "Grass is dying" | Irrigation issue, soil compaction, pests, or disease — check irrigation, aerate soil, inspect for pests |
| "Lawn is brown" | Drought stress, grub damage, or fungal disease — adjust irrigation, check for grubs, treat fungus |
| "Weeds everywhere" | Poor turf health, lack of pre-emergent, or soil imbalance — apply herbicide, improve turf density, mulch beds |
| "Tree is dead/dying" | Disease, pest infestation, root damage, or environmental stress — assess tree health, remove if hazardous |
| "Branch is hanging" | Storm damage or dead branch — prune branch, check for decay |
| "Hedge is overgrown" | Lack of regular trimming — trim hedge, shape to spec |
| "Flowers are wilting" | Watering issue, poor drainage, or sun exposure — check watering, soil drainage, sun exposure |
| "Soil is hard/compacted" | Soil compaction from foot traffic or clay — aerate soil, amend with compost |
| "Moss on lawn" | Poor drainage, excessive shade, or low soil pH — improve drainage, reduce shade, apply moss killer, test pH |
| "Leaves everywhere" | Seasonal leaf drop — rake and remove leaf litter |
| "Sprinkler not working" | Broken head, clogged emitter, or valve issue — inspect heads, check valves, clear clogs |
| "Standing water" | Poor drainage or grading issue — improve drainage, regrade area |
| "Grass is patchy" | Thin turf, poor seed germination, or pest damage — overseed, fertilize, improve soil |
| "Plants are dying" | Improper planting, watering issues, or disease — check planting depth, watering schedule, inspect for disease |

### Key Terminology
- **Aeration**: Process of punching holes in soil to improve air/water penetration
- **Overseeding**: Spreading seed over existing lawn to thicken turf
- **Topdressing**: Applying thin layer of compost/soil over lawn
- **Mulch**: Protective layer over soil (wood chips, bark, straw)
- **Hardscape**: Non-plant elements (patios, walkways, retaining walls)
- **Softscape**: Plant elements (trees, shrubs, flowers, lawn)
- **Xeriscaping**: Water-efficient landscaping with drought-tolerant plants
- **Drip irrigation**: Water-efficient system delivering water directly to roots
- **Emitter**: Drip irrigation component that releases water
- **Backflow preventer**: Device preventing irrigation water from contaminating potable supply
- **Soil pH**: Acidity/alkalinity level (6.0-7.0 ideal for most plants)
- **USDA Hardiness Zone**: Geographic zone based on minimum winter temperature
- **Pre-emergent**: Herbicide applied before weed seeds germinate
- **Post-emergent**: Herbicide applied to actively growing weeds
- **Dormancy**: Period of reduced plant growth (winter for cool-season, summer for warm-season)
- **Transplant shock**: Stress after moving a plant

### Tools
Mower, string trimmer (weed eater), hedge trimmer, leaf blower, shovel, rake, aerator, sprayer, chainsaw, wheelbarrow, pruning shears, loppers, garden fork, trowel

### Safety
- PPE for chemical application: gloves, goggles, long sleeves, respirator
- 10ft clearance from overhead power lines when pruning
- Steel-toed boots, gloves, eye protection
- Hearing protection for mowers, trimmers, chainsaws
- Proper lifting technique — bend knees, not back
- Sun protection — hat, sunscreen, hydration
- Never mow wet grass or on steep slopes
- Keep children and pets inside during mowing
- Chainsaw safety: never cut above shoulder height, avoid kickback zone

### Permits
- Required for: retaining walls >2ft, fences >4ft, pools, tree removal (some areas), grading changes >24", projects >2,500 sq ft
- Not required for: planting, mulching, mowing, minor landscaping
- Cost: $20-$700 depending on scope
- Pesticide applicator license required for commercial chemical application

### Pricing
- Hourly: $25-$75/hr
- Per project: $100-$5,000+
- Per sq ft: $4.50-$17
- Lawn mowing: $30-$80 per visit
- Tree removal: $200-$2,000+
- Landscape design: $100-$300 consultation

### Seasonal
- **Spring**: Cleanup, planting, pre-emergent, fertilization, irrigation startup
- **Summer**: Maintenance, watering, mowing, pest control, deadheading
- **Fall**: BEST planting season (roots establish in cool soil), overseeding, leaf removal, winterization
- **Winter**: Dormancy, planning, pruning (for some species), equipment maintenance
- Fall is ideal for planting trees/shrubs — warm soil + cool air = root growth without transplant shock

## 6. GENERAL CONTRACTING

### Common Homeowner Complaints → Trade Translation
| Homeowner Says | Trade Term / What It Means |
|---|---|
| "Door won't close" | Hinge misalignment, latch issue, or frame shift — adjust hinges, check latch, inspect frame |
| "Window is broken" | Glass failure, sash damage, or seal failure — replace glass/sash, check seal |
| "Paint is peeling" | Moisture issue, poor prep, or incompatible paint — scrape, prime, repaint with proper prep |
| "Wall has a crack" | Settlement, structural issue, or drywall seam — assess crack, patch, monitor |
| "Floor is creaking" | Loose subfloor or joist movement — secure subfloor, add screws/joist hangers |
| "Cabinet is loose" | Failed mounting or wall damage — re-secure cabinet, repair wall |
| "Lock is broken" | Cylinder failure or mechanism damage — replace lock cylinder/mechanism |
| "Screen is torn" | Mesh damage or frame failure — replace screen mesh/frame |
| "Carpet is stained" | Deep stain or fiber damage — clean or replace carpet section |
| "Tile is cracked" | Substrate movement or impact damage — replace tile, check substrate |
| "Project is taking too long" | Scheduling delay, material delay, or scope change — review timeline, check material orders |
| "Cost is higher than quoted" | Change orders, unforeseen conditions, or scope creep — review change orders, verify scope |
| "Work quality is poor" | Substandard workmanship — document issues, request correction |
| "Contractor stopped showing up" | Abandoned project — document, contact licensing board, seek legal advice |

### Key Terminology
- **Scope of work**: Detailed description of work to be performed
- **Change order**: Written modification to original contract (cost/scope change)
- **Punch list**: List of remaining items to complete at end of project
- **Lien waiver**: Document releasing right to place lien on property
- **Surety bond**: Financial guarantee protecting against contractor default
- **General liability insurance**: Covers property damage and injuries
- **Workers' comp**: Covers worker injuries on job site
- **Subcontractor**: Trade specialist hired by GC (plumber, electrician, etc.)
- **Prime contract**: Agreement between owner and GC
- **Retainage**: Percentage of payment held until project completion
- **Rough-in**: Initial stage of mechanical/electrical/plumbing before walls closed
- **Finish**: Final stage of work (trim, paint, fixtures)
- **Punch list**: Final walkthrough items to complete
- **Certificate of occupancy**: Official approval that building is safe to occupy

### Tools
Varies by trade — GC coordinates all trades. Common: measuring tools, project management software, safety equipment, communication devices

### Safety
- Verify contractor is licensed, insured, and bonded
- Written contract REQUIRED — never work without one
- Never pay in full upfront — typical: 1/3 deposit, 1/3 progress, 1/3 completion
- 3-day cooling-off rule for door-to-door contracts
- Stop work immediately for safety hazards (exposed wiring, gas smell, structural instability)
- Document everything — photos, texts, emails, receipts
- Verify permits are pulled before work starts
- Request lien waivers from subcontractors

### Permits
- GC typically responsible for obtaining all required permits
- Required for: structural changes, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, additions, decks, fences
- Cost: varies widely by jurisdiction and project scope
- Unpermitted work can cause insurance claim denials and sale complications

### Pricing
- GC fee: 10-20% of total project cost
- **Time & Materials (T&M)**: Actual hours + materials + markup — risk on owner
- **Fixed-price (lump sum)**: Set price regardless of actual cost — risk on contractor
- **Unit price**: Per unit of work (per sq ft, per linear foot) — common in commercial
- Material markup: 15-30% above invoice cost
- Overhead: 20-35% of project cost
- Profit margin: 3-8% commercial, 10-15% residential

### Seasonal
- **Winter (Jan-Mar)**: BEST for interior work — 10-20% lower prices, more scheduling flexibility
- **Spring (Apr-Jun)**: Peak season — highest prices, longest wait times (8-12 weeks)
- **Summer (Jul-Aug)**: Peak demand — good for outdoor work, expensive for all projects
- **Fall (Sep-Nov)**: Good for exterior work — moderate prices, good weather, pre-winter urgency
- Plan in fall, permit in winter, build in late winter/early spring for best pricing

## Cross-Trade Safety Themes

1. **PPE is non-negotiable**: Hard hats, safety glasses, steel-toed boots, gloves, hearing protection
2. **Lockout/tagout**: De-energize and verify before working on electrical/mechanical systems
3. **Fall protection**: Required for work at heights >6 feet (OSHA)
4. **Permits protect homeowners**: Unpermitted work = insurance/sale/liability issues
5. **Written contracts**: Never start work without a signed, detailed contract
6. **Stop work authority**: Workers should stop for safety hazards without retaliation
7. **Documentation**: Photos, receipts, contracts, change orders — document everything
8. **3-day cooling-off**: Door-to-door contracts can be cancelled within 3 days
9. **Never pay in full upfront**: Progress payments tied to completion milestones
10. **Verify credentials**: License, insurance, bonding — always verify before hiring
